## ⚡ Energy — oil

**A Hormuz deal that still lets Tehran hold the toll gate.** Iran and Oman said
Wednesday they'd reached an agreement on a proposed transit route through the
Strait of Hormuz — inbound ships through Iranian waters, outbound through
Oman's — but crude reversed higher Thursday after Iran's state news agency
published a draft plan with far more restrictive conditions than traders had
priced. WTI traded near **$77.75/bbl** and Brent near **$83.34/bbl** on 6
August, both up on the day but still down roughly **10% on the week** on
reopening hopes.
*Risk lens:* traders remember June's short-lived MOU collapsing within days —
until sustained, verifiable transits are confirmed rather than announced,
positioning for a "reopening" is positioning for a headline, not a supply
change.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/iran-says-agreement-on-hormuz-shipping-route-reached-with-oman) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/oil-price-iran-war-strait-hormuz-oman-deal.html)

**Shipowners balk at Iran's proposed transit fees.** Reporting on the draft
Hormuz framework shows Tehran's plan would require vessels to pay fees to
transit the strait it would now coordinate — a toll on roughly a fifth of
global daily oil and LNG flows that shipowners have publicly objected to.
*Risk lens:* a fee-and-permission regime turns a chokepoint into a standing
cost and counterparty exposure even after the shooting stops — freight and
war-risk premia don't reprice to pre-war levels just because tankers are
moving again.
— [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/06/iran-oman-deal-strait-of-hormuz-shipowners-fees/)

## 🔥 Gas & LNG

**TTF and Henry Hub keep pulling in opposite directions.** European gas rose
to **€53.62/MWh** on 6 August, up 2.3% on the day as the market stayed
unconvinced the Hormuz draft plan de-escalates near-term risk. US Henry Hub
fell to **$2.67/MMBtu** the same day, pinned down by ample production and
storage.
*Risk lens:* the same headline is pushing the two benchmarks apart, not
together — a US-hub hedge still does nothing for a European supply exposure,
and the basis is the trade here, not the flat price.
— [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/eu-natural-gas) · [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/ng/hist/rngwhhdm.htm)

## 🪙 Metals

**Copper prints an all-time high on Congo's export ban.** Copper jumped to a
record **~$6.71/lb** on 6 August after the Democratic Republic of Congo
officially banned copper (and cobalt) concentrate exports to force more
domestic processing.
*Risk lens:* this is a concentrate-availability shock, not a demand story —
smelters and refiners short concentrate feel it first, and the refined-versus-
concentrate basis is where the stress shows up before flat price fully
reflects it.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/copper-jumps-to-its-highest-level-ever-what-the-metal-is-telling-us-.html)

**Gold hits a seven-week high on a weak ADP print and Hormuz de-escalation
hopes.** Spot gold reached as high as **$4,295/oz** before settling near
**$4,268/oz** on 6 August, after ADP reported private payrolls grew just
**44,000** in July versus expectations, well below consensus. September
rate-hike odds eased to **55%** from 67% two days earlier.
*Risk lens:* gold is rallying on a dovish jobs signal and a Middle East
de-risking narrative at the same time — two drivers that don't normally point
the same way. Today's payrolls print can flip either one and gold's
positioning is stretched into it.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/gold-price-payrolls-data-hormuz-deal.html) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/gold-holds-gain-as-progress-on-hormuz-deal-lowers-rate-hike-odds)

## 📊 Macro & cross-asset

**July's nonfarm payrolls land today with the bar already lowered.** ADP's
**44,000** private-payrolls miss on 5 August followed June's soft **57,000**
BLS print, and consensus for today's official July number centers near
**83,000** with unemployment steady at **4.2%** — though estimates range as
low as 18,000. The dollar and yen have been drifting into the release on both
payroll jitters and Hormuz-deal uncertainty.
*Risk lens:* this print lands two weeks after a Fed hold that carried a
three-member hawkish dissent. A weak number reopens the cut debate the
dissenters were fighting against; a strong one reopens the hike case — either
way, positioning built around the "hold" consensus is exposed today.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/the-july-jobs-numbers-are-due-out-friday-heres-what-to-expect.html) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/05/private-companies-added-just-44000-workers-in-july-below-expectations-adp-reports.html)
