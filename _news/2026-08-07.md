## ⚡ Energy — oil

**A Hormuz deal is forming, but it comes with strings attached.** Iran and
Oman have agreed in principle on a proposed shipping route through the
Strait, with a joint statement expected soon "provided certain third parties
do not obstruct the process." Tehran's own account is more conditional than
the market's relief-rally reading of it: Iran wants US and Israeli vessels
barred outright, and compensation from other "hostile" countries before
they're allowed through. Brent traded near **$79.47/bbl** in Thursday's
session, well off the $90 highs of late July.
*Risk lens:* a deal that reopens the strait for some flags and not others
isn't a return to normal transit — it's a new, discretionary chokepoint with
Tehran holding the toggle. Pricing this as "Hormuz is fixed" misprices the
optionality that's still sitting with one counterparty.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/iran-says-agreement-on-hormuz-shipping-route-reached-with-oman) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/iran-seeks-bar-on-us-ships-in-hormuz-as-deal-with-oman-advances)

**The UAE quietly became the war's shock absorber.** ADNOC has moved more
crude through Hormuz than any other producer over the past two months —
the tracked figure is likely a floor, given untracked "dark" crossings.
Meanwhile the supermajors are positioning their capital allocation for
lower prices ahead, not higher.
*Risk lens:* a single producer absorbing a disproportionate share of
chokepoint risk is a concentration the freight and war-risk insurance
markets haven't fully repriced. If ADNOC's flow is what's holding the
global balance together, that's the number to watch, not the headline
Brent print.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/uae-s-adnoc-defies-hormuz-risks-to-keep-crude-flowing-to-global-market) · [Bloomberg Opinion](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-06/shell-bp-exxon-big-oil-is-bracing-for-lower-prices-and-rightly-so)

## 📊 Macro & cross-asset

**Gold's best day in six months says the market doesn't trust the new Fed
chair's independence.** Gold jumped over **4.3%** on Wednesday to above
**$4,600/oz**, its strongest gain since February, as Hormuz de-escalation
and a weakening dollar combined with growing doubt that Chairman Kevin
Warsh will tighten policy despite elevated inflation. The 10-year Treasury
yield eased to around **4.60%** from 4.75% a week earlier. Central banks
bought a record 289 tonnes of gold in Q2 — up 62% year-on-year.
*Risk lens:* gold rallying on *less* central-bank credibility, not more
safe-haven demand, is a different trade than it looks like on the surface.
A book hedged for "risk-off gold" is not hedged for "policy-credibility
gold" — the correlations to real yields and the dollar behave differently
in each regime.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/gold-holds-gain-as-progress-on-hormuz-deal-lowers-rate-hike-odds)

**The "Bessent-Warsh double whammy" is reopening the Sell America trade —
in bonds and the dollar, not yet equities.** Warsh's sparse communication
at last week's press conference cast doubt on the Fed's inflation
commitment; separately, Treasury Secretary Bessent signed off on US
support to help Japan prop up the yen, an intervention that itself
pressures the dollar. Treasury yields have risen to the highest since 2007
even as the S&P 500 sits at a record on a tech rally.
*Risk lens:* when the policy mix pushes bonds and the dollar one way while
equities go the other, cross-asset correlation assumptions break first —
and a portfolio's stock-bond hedge is exactly the thing most likely to stop
working when it's needed.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/us-policies-reignite-debate-over-sell-america-trade-strategy) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-said-to-call-warsh-in-latest-signal-of-push-to-remake-fed)

## 🪙 Metals

**The aluminum tariff is now a domestic price problem, not a trade one.**
Canadian PM Mark Carney said Trump's 50% tariff on aluminum imports is
mostly being paid by US buyers: the metal's producer price index rose
**52%** year-on-year since the tariff took effect last June. Carney says a
"win-win" trade deal is possible if metals, autos and lumber are addressed
together.
*Risk lens:* a tariff that shows up almost entirely in the domestic PPI,
not in reduced import volumes, is a pass-through cost sitting on downstream
buyers' books right now — it belongs in the input-cost hedge, not the
trade-policy watch list.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/carney-says-aluminum-prices-open-way-to-trade-deal-with-trump)

**Indonesia still hasn't decided how radical its commodity export overhaul
will be.** Nearly three months after President Prabowo proposed centralising
control of exports to curb under-invoicing, officials remain split between
his sweeping vision and a narrower monitoring-only mandate. Indonesia is the
top global exporter of thermal coal, nickel and palm oil; the first
implementation test lands next month. Separately, Glencore plans a
secondary Australian listing as soon as October to tap the country's
A$4.4 trillion pension pool for copper and M&A capital.
*Risk lens:* prolonged policy ambiguity from the top exporter of three
separate commodities is a supply-side tail risk that doesn't show up in
any single futures curve — it sits in counterparty and documentation risk
on physical contracts, which is much harder to hedge than price.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/prabowo-s-radical-commodity-export-plan-faces-dueling-visions) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/glencore-eyes-australia-listing-to-tap-3-1-trillion-pension-pot)
