**US drivers are paying more for fuel this late in the year than they ever
have.** Gasoline is selling above $4 a gallon and diesel near $5.40, against
$3.16 and $3.72 twelve months ago — records for the season. More than five
months after the Iran war began choking flows through the Strait of Hormuz,
the US Department of Energy raised its forecasts again this week, projecting
gasoline to average $4 a gallon in the third quarter and $3.72 in the fourth,
with diesel still just shy of $5 by year end.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-13/us-fuel-prices-will-likely-stay-high-for-a-while) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/americans-have-never-paid-this-much-for-fuel-so-late-in-the-year) · [AAA](https://gasprices.aaa.com/)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (product cracks)** — an official forecast that keeps
diesel near $5 through year end is a floor under distillate cracks, not a
ceiling. Hedges struck on crude flat price will keep under-performing the
product exposure they were meant to cover; the basis between crude and the
distillate barrel is where the unhedged risk now sits.

**Russian diesel exports have collapsed to a fraction of last year's, and it
is a refining problem, not a sanctions one.** Diesel and gasoil shipments ran
at about 80,000 barrels a day over the first seven days of August, against more
than a million a day at the end of last year. Ukrainian drone strikes are the
cause: Russian refinery runs fell to roughly 3.6 million barrels a day in July,
the lowest since May 2002, with at least 25 refineries hit since August 2025.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/russian-diesel-flows-are-lowest-in-years-as-drones-hit-refiners) · [The Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/03/russian-oil-refining-falls-to-24-year-low-after-ukrainian-drone-strikes-bloomberg-a93404) · [Kpler](https://www.kpler.com/blog/ukraines-drone-campaign-pushes-russian-refinery-runs-to-21-year-lows)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (distillate)** — this is structural supply destruction
with no quick repair path, layered on top of the Hormuz disruption. A country
moving from major exporter toward importer of refined product removes a swing
supplier from the global balance. Anyone short distillate cracks as a hedge
against a crude rally is carrying the wrong leg.

**Crude itself has been drifting lower, which is the part that does not fit
the narrative.** Brent traded around $87 a barrel on Thursday, roughly 2%
lower on the day, despite no visible progress toward reopening the strait.
Traders are openly disputing how much crude is actually transiting: US Energy
Secretary Chris Wright put the figure near 9 million barrels a day, a number
the market met with scepticism, while the IEA warns global stockpiles are
drawing down fast.
— [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/13/world/live-news/iran-war-trump) · [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/12/oil-prices-rise-as-attacks-dent-hopes-for-strait-of-hormuz-reopening)

*Risk lens:* **Neutral (Brent flat price)** — flat price is being held down by
stock draws and higher US output while the underlying chokepoint risk is
unresolved. That is a compressed-volatility setup on an unhedged tail: cheap
optionality relative to the event risk, and a poor environment for short-vol
positions on crude.
