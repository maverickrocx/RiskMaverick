## ⚡ Energy — oil

**Iran rules out direct Hormuz talks, demands compensation first.** Brent for
October delivery rose about 1.1% to **$84.46/bbl** and WTI for September gained
0.8% to **$78.84** on Monday, as Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran is
not in direct talks with Washington to reopen the Strait despite US claims a
deal is close, and that Iran wants compensation as a condition of reopening.
The Strait remains effectively closed, and Houthi attacks are now disrupting
the Red Sea alternative route too.
*Risk lens:* two negotiating tracks — Iran-US and the Iran-Oman transit
arrangement — are stalled at the same time, closing off the fallback route
along with the primary one. Freight and war-risk cover priced for "deal
imminent" is priced for the wrong scenario.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz-trump-iran.html)

**A Houthi drone hit an Aramco refinery in Jizan.** Saudi authorities confirmed
a fire at the Jizan refinery on 9 August; Aramco's industrial firefighting
teams extinguished it and no injuries were reported. Houthi military
spokesman Yahya Saree said the group struck the site with a drone in response
to Saudi-enabled strikes on Houthi-held Yemen.
*Risk lens:* a second live front on Gulf energy risk — Yemen's 2022 truce
fraying alongside the Hormuz standoff — and this one lands directly on Saudi
downstream infrastructure rather than just shipping lanes.
— [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/saudi-arabia-says-fire-extinguished-at-aramco-facility-in-jizan) · [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/09/nx-s1-5926387/yemens-houthis-claim-attack-on-aramco-oil-facility-in-saudi-arabia-and-other-middle-east-news)

## 🔥 Gas & LNG

**TTF has cooled even as oil stays jumpy on the same Hormuz standoff.**
European front-month gas closed at **€55.55/MWh** on 7 August, down from
**€59.44** a week earlier on 31 July — a roughly 6.5% pullback even as Brent
and WTI held their Hormuz-driven premium.
*Risk lens:* gas and oil are supposed to move together on a shared chokepoint
story, and right now they aren't. A Hormuz hedge sized off the oil curve
won't capture what the gas curve is actually doing.
— [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/eu-natural-gas)

## 🔌 Power & carbon

**EUAs hit a seven-week high.** EU carbon permits rose 1.7% to **€83.29/t** on
7 August, their best level in seven weeks, as the market keeps digesting the
European Commission's July ETS reform package — a slower cap decline, a
reworked Market Stability Reserve and expanded industrial support — read by
traders as more supportive than the prior tightening-only trajectory.
*Risk lens:* a policy-driven high, not a demand-driven one — carbon is
running on cap-and-reform mechanics right now, not the energy complex, so
treat carbon and gas/power exposures as moving on different clocks.
— [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/carbon)

## 🪙 Metals

**Copper is trading like a stockout, not a growth story.** LME cash copper
now commands a premium above **$100/tonne** over the three-month contract —
the widest backwardation since January, up from about $30 a week earlier —
while Comex futures touched a record **$6.70/lb** (about $14,781/tonne). More
than 200,000 tonnes of copper arrived at US ports in July, the largest
monthly inflow on record, as traders keep front-running a still-pending US
tariff decision on refined copper.
*Risk lens:* backwardation this steep is a physical-scarcity signal, not a
macro one — a hedge built around demand fundamentals misses the real driver,
which is tariff positioning draining the LME network. Basis risk between LME
and Comex is the live exposure.
— [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/copper-price-sets-fresh-us-record-as-tariff-driven-hoarding-meets-shrinking-supply/) · [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/copper-price-tops-14000-as-us-stockpiles-swell-before-tariff-call/)

**Gold's bull case is intact, but the forecasts are being trimmed.** A Reuters
poll of 29 analysts and traders cut the median 2026 gold forecast to
**$4,509/oz**, down from $4,916 three months ago — the first downward
revision in 11 quarters — after the metal's worst quarterly drawdown since
2013 pulled it well off January's **$5,595** record. Spot gold was still
trading near **$4,343/oz** on 7 August, up 2.44% on the day.
*Risk lens:* even the bulls are cutting numbers while central banks keep
buying (a net 289 tonnes in Q2) — "structural support" and "further upside"
are two different claims, and books still sized for a run at $5,000+ should
check which one they're actually hedging for.
— [Kitco News](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-07-29/gold-forecasts-fall-central-bank-buying-expected-cushion-retreat) · [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/gold)

## 📊 Macro & cross-asset

**Equities open a record-high, CPI-heavy week.** The S&P 500 closed at an
all-time high of **7,757.64** on 7 August, capping its best week since April
on a dovish reaction to a weak July jobs report. This week brings July CPI on
Wednesday (consensus +0.2% headline, +0.3% core), PPI on Thursday and retail
sales on Friday, alongside AI-capex reads from Cisco's and Applied Materials'
earnings.
*Risk lens:* a record struck on rate-cut hopes is one a single hot CPI print
can unwind fast — books running long duration and long equity beta on the
same "soft landing" read are carrying the same risk twice.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-aug-10-14-2026-.html)
