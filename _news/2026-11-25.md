## Oil / Products {#oil-products}

*Sourced from Bloomberg's Energy Daily.*

**The IEA now sees a 2 million-barrel-a-day surplus this quarter and Bernstein up to 2.5 million next year, as Gulf exports near prewar levels and benchmark crude has fallen more than a fifth this month.** — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-24/the-oil-market-is-talking-about-a-surplus-again) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/global-oil-market-swings-to-surplus-on-gulf-exports-iea-says)

*Risk lens:* **Bearish (Brent flat price)** — the war premium is unwinding, but depleted inventories mean any new supply shock would hit harder, so keep upside optionality rather than going naked short.

**Chinese state and independent refiners stepped up crude purchases this month, using cheaper oil and freight to refill stockpiles drained by the Iran war.** — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/chinese-refiners-snapping-up-cut-price-oil-to-refill-stockpiles)

*Risk lens:* **Neutral (Asian crude differentials)** — restocking demand puts a soft floor under Middle East grades even as the headline balance loosens.

## LNG {#lng}

*Sourced from Bloomberg's Energy Daily.*

**Japan's physical spark spread for spot LNG turned positive on a seven-day average for the first time since the Iran war began, setting up more spot cargo buying.** — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/japan-lng-buying-poised-to-rise-as-gas-power-turns-profitable)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (prompt JKM)** — returning Japanese spot demand competes with Europe for winter cargoes, widening the JKM–TTF spread risk.
