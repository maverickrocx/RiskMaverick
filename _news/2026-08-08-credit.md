*Sourced from this morning's Bloomberg newsletter.*

**Credit investors are repricing the hyperscaler AI buildout before equity
investors are.** Alphabet raised $25 billion in its latest mega bond sale
this week, on top of more than $50 billion of debt already issued in the
first half of the year, drawing about $115 billion of orders — still
strong demand, though it took fresh deal concessions to get there. Look at
the bonds already outstanding, though: spreads on the 30-year debt
Alphabet sold in February have widened from about 83 basis points at
issuance to over 100bp now, having been as low as 70bp back in June. As
recently as 2024 Alphabet barely touched the bond market; it's reportedly
telling investors it will now be a regular issuer, more like a bank than a
tech company.
*Risk lens:* stocks are near record highs while credit spreads on the same
company's debt are widening — a divergence that shows up in credit before
it shows up in equity. A hyperscaler business model that's flipped from
cash-rich to debt-funded almost overnight changes the correlation between
AI-capex names' equity and credit risk, and portfolios exposed to both
should treat that as a live basis-risk question, not a rounding error.
