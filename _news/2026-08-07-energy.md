*Sourced from this morning's Bloomberg and Economist newsletters.*

**Hormuz is being legislated, not reopened.** Iran's parliament is reviewing a
draft plan — reported by the Iranian news site Fars — that would formally ban
vessels from America, Israel and other "hostile" countries from transiting
the Strait, and fine violators up to 20% of their cargo's value. Oil prices
rose slightly on the news. This sits alongside the Iran-Oman shipping route
agreed in principle earlier in the week, which Tehran has cast in the same
conditional terms: US and Israeli vessels barred, compensation demanded from
others. Brent traded near **$79.47/bbl** in Thursday's session, well off the
$90 highs of late July.
*Risk lens:* a chokepoint with published rules of entry isn't the same as an
open one — it's a toll booth with a political filter. Pricing this as "Hormuz
is fixed" misprices the optionality that's still sitting entirely with
Tehran.

**The UAE quietly became the war's shock absorber.** ADNOC has moved more
crude through Hormuz than any other producer over the past two months — the
tracked figure is likely a floor, given untracked "dark" crossings.
Meanwhile the supermajors are positioning their capital allocation for lower
prices ahead, not higher.
*Risk lens:* a single producer absorbing a disproportionate share of
chokepoint risk is a concentration the freight and war-risk insurance
markets haven't fully repriced. If ADNOC's flow is what's holding the global
balance together, that's the number to watch, not the headline Brent print.
