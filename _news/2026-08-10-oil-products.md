*Sourced from Bloomberg's Energy Daily.*

**Trump has swapped an imminent peace deal for a waiting game, and the oil
market is the one carrying the cost of the delay.** In little over a week the
US President moved from touting a near-term agreement with Iran to a "low key"
posture, betting that economic pressure and a naval blockade will bring Tehran
to terms without fresh strikes. Millions of barrels still leave the Persian
Gulf, largely via a furtive network of shuttle tankers running the strait
undetected. Crude sits well below the highs struck at the start of the
conflict, helped by China's demand adjustment and rising output across the
Americas.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-10/trump-s-low-key-pivot-leaves-hormuz-oil-in-limbo) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/trump-hints-us-will-let-economic-pressure-on-iran-do-the-work)

*Risk lens:* **Neutral (flat price), bullish (tail)** — a policy of waiting
converts a resolvable event into an open-ended one. Flat price is calm because
the shortfall is being absorbed, not because it has gone away. That is the
setup where realised vol understates the risk actually being run.

**The buffers absorbing the shortfall are close to spent.** World oil
inventories are drawing down briskly again after a pause during the ceasefire,
and America's Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest since the 1980s.
The squeeze is worst in refined product: diesel is trading above $160 a barrel
in Europe, with Russian supply simultaneously constrained by Ukrainian strikes
on its refineries. The market stays exposed to sporadic spikes of the kind
that briefly took crude to $100 last month.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-08/war-brings-winter-of-discontent-for-the-world-s-workhorse-fuel) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-10/trump-s-low-key-pivot-leaves-hormuz-oil-in-limbo)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (distillate)** — a depleted SPR removes the shock
absorber that has capped previous spikes. With inventory cover thinning into
winter, the asymmetry sits firmly on the upside for products, and any hedge
built on crude flat price leaves the crack exposed.

**The physical infrastructure keeps getting hit while the diplomacy stalls.**
The UAE — which has moved more crude through Hormuz than any other producer
over the past two months — said a vessel was targeted by a missile on
Saturday, following three struck the previous week. The next day Saudi Arabia
extinguished a fire at its Jazan refinery on the Red Sea, which the Houthis
claimed to have attacked. In Russia, a Ukrainian drone strike on the
Nizhnekamsk refining and petrochemical hub in Tatarstan killed 13 and injured
nearly 40.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/adnoc-says-three-vessels-hit-this-week-while-transiting-hormuz) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/russia-says-13-killed-in-ukraine-attack-on-tatarstan-energy-hub)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (freight and war risk)** — attacks on the largest
remaining user of the chokepoint price war-risk premia directly. Anyone
holding term freight or relying on a single routing through Hormuz is carrying
concentration risk that a flat-price hedge does not touch.
