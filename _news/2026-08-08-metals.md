*Sourced from this morning's Bloomberg newsletter.*

**Copper's last-resort buffer is running dry.** LME futures pushed past
$14,000 a ton this week, a level breached on only a handful of days this
year, with some traders eyeing the record $14,500-plus touched during
January's speculative buying in China. Readily available LME stocks have
fallen to just 92,900 tons — enough to cover the 27-million-ton-a-year
global copper market for a little over a day. The squeeze is being driven
from both ends: US imports are surging as traders capture arbitrage ahead
of a possible Trump tariff, while Chinese buyers are ordering more
imported copper as domestic ore and scrap supply tightens. Previous drops
below the 100,000-ton threshold have triggered spot-price spikes before.
*Risk lens:* this rally is a stockout signal, not a demand signal — it's
being driven by trade dislocation and warehouse drawdowns, not a
brightening growth outlook. Hedges built around "copper as a growth
bellwether" will misread this move; the more relevant risk right now is
basis risk between LME futures and physical spot if the warehouse buffer
keeps draining.
