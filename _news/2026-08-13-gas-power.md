*Sourced from Bloomberg's Green Daily.*

**Europe's most important inland waterway is running dry with no relief in
sight.** Levels at Kaub, the Rhine's shallowest point, have fallen to the
lowest since records began in 1880, and may drop further as another heat wave
bakes the continent this week. Forecasters say weeks of sustained rain would be
needed to return the river to normal, meaning the crisis could drag into
October. The Rhine carries freight through Europe's industrial heartland from
Switzerland to Rotterdam, and the disruption is already reaching steelmaker
Thyssenkrupp, BASF and Lanxess. Lanxess said vessels are carrying significantly
lighter loads and that with some loading points inaccessible it is switching to
rail and road where it can, calling the situation "highly dynamic".
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/rhine-river-at-record-low-levels-is-forcing-industry-to-react)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (German power and industrial input costs)** — low Rhine
levels hit energy twice: barge-delivered coal and fuel oil get more expensive
per tonne as vessels part-load, and the same heat suppressing the river cuts
hydro and thermal cooling capacity. This is a logistics constraint expressing
itself as a power-price and industrial-margin event, and it lands on a German
gas position already behind on storage. For anyone with delivered-basis supply
contracts on the Rhine corridor, freight is the exposure that reprices first —
and it is rarely the one that is hedged.
