*Sourced from this morning's Bloomberg, Economist and Moneycontrol newsletters.*

**Gold's best day in six months says the market doesn't trust the new Fed
chair's independence.** Gold jumped over **4.3%** on Wednesday to above
**$4,600/oz**, its strongest gain since February, as Hormuz de-escalation and
a weakening dollar combined with growing doubt that Chairman Kevin Warsh
will tighten policy despite elevated inflation. The 10-year Treasury yield
eased to around **4.60%** from 4.75% a week earlier. Central banks bought a
record 289 tonnes of gold in Q2 — up 62% year-on-year. The Economist frames
the same question from the other side: what does Warsh actually do if the
data forces his hand?
*Risk lens:* gold rallying on *less* central-bank credibility, not more
safe-haven demand, is a different trade than it looks like on the surface. A
book hedged for "risk-off gold" is not hedged for "policy-credibility gold"
— the correlations to real yields and the dollar behave differently in each
regime.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/gold-holds-gain-as-progress-on-hormuz-deal-lowers-rate-hike-odds) · [Economist](https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2026/08/02/what-will-kevin-warsh-do-if-americas-economy-breaks)

**The "Bessent-Warsh double whammy" is reopening the Sell America trade — in
bonds and the dollar, not yet equities.** Warsh's sparse communication at
last week's press conference cast doubt on the Fed's inflation commitment;
separately, Treasury Secretary Bessent signed off on US support to help
Japan prop up the yen, an intervention that itself pressures the dollar.
Treasury yields have risen to the highest since 2007 even as the S&P 500
sits at a record on a tech rally.
*Risk lens:* when the policy mix pushes bonds and the dollar one way while
equities go the other, cross-asset correlation assumptions break first — and
a portfolio's stock-bond hedge is exactly the thing most likely to stop
working when it's needed.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/us-policies-reignite-debate-over-sell-america-trade-strategy) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-said-to-call-warsh-in-latest-signal-of-push-to-remake-fed)

**The RBI held its repo rate at 5.25% — and flagged that real rates are
about to go negative.** The Monetary Policy Committee voted unanimously to
hold, trimming its retail inflation projection by 10bp and lifting its FY27
growth forecast by 10bp. Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the current inflation
pressure is supply-side — food and fuel, largely transmitted through the
Iran war — not demand-driven, and that core inflation remains moderate. With
inflation projected to peak near **5.9%** in Q4 against a 5.25% repo rate,
the real policy rate is set to turn negative for the best part of a year.
*Risk lens:* a central bank choosing to look through supply-side inflation
is making a bet that the shock is temporary — the same bet the Fed is being
second-guessed on right now, just with the RBI naming the mechanism (Iran,
energy) explicitly instead of leaving it implicit.
