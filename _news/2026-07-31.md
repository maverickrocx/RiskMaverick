## ⚡ Energy — oil

**Brent and WTI head for a ~21% monthly surge.** After a week that swung more than $8 a barrel, Brent settled near **$89.03** and WTI near **$83.59** on Thursday (30 July) as the US-Iran conflict ground on; Bloomberg put July on track for crude's biggest monthly gain since March.
*Risk lens:* the range, not the level, is the story this month — a book hedged to the month-open price has already absorbed an $8 intraday round trip once this week alone.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-july-31) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/oil-prices-us-iran-war.html)

**A second chokepoint is now live: the Black Sea.** Two more tankers near the Caspian Pipeline Consortium's Novorossiysk terminal were struck overnight into 30 July, Bloomberg reported — the latest in a run of drone attacks that first forced CPC to suspend loadings on 19 July, repeatedly cutting off Kazakhstan's main export route.
*Risk lens:* freight and war-risk insurance premiums on Black Sea liftings should be marked to the current disruption, not assumed static alongside the Hormuz story.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-july-31) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-19/caspian-pipeline-consortium-halts-loadings-after-drone-strike)

**Saudi Arabia pushes a 40-country naval coalition.** Riyadh's defence ministry said more than 40 nations joined talks on protecting Gulf and Red Sea shipping lanes, after Iran-aligned Houthis declared a maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia and Iran continued targeting Hormuz traffic.
*Risk lens:* a credible escort framework would compress the war-risk premium currently embedded in freight and insurance costs — worth watching for follow-through rather than trading the announcement itself.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/oil-prices-us-iran-war.html)

## 🔥 Gas & LNG

**Henry Hub sits at a three-month low.** The prompt month fell to roughly **$2.70–2.76/MMBtu**, pressured by record Lower-48 production (~110.6 bcfd) and storage running about 6.4% above the five-year seasonal average.
— [EIA STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/natgas.php) · [American Gas Association](https://www.aga.org/research-policy/resource-library/natural-gas-market-indicators-july-23-2026/)

**A Qatari LNG cargo finally clears Hormuz.** The QatarEnergy-controlled tanker *Al Areesh* exited the strait on 29 July — the first such transit since 11 July — and is now bound for Pakistan, a tentative sign Gulf LNG traffic is resuming after weeks of disruption.
*Risk lens:* one transit does not restore the corridor; buyers exposed to Qatari cargoes should keep treating delivery timing as at risk until transits become routine again.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/qatar-sends-its-first-lng-shipment-through-hormuz-in-three-weeks)

## 🔌 Power & carbon

**EUAs hold just above €80.** The contract traded in a **€79.4–82/t** range through July, buoyed by positioning ahead of the European Commission's 17 July Phase 5 ETS proposal, which would slow the annual cap-reduction rate and extend free allocation to 2037 for decarbonisation investors.
*Risk lens:* policy is setting this price, not power burn — carbon hedges should track the reform timeline as closely as the demand curve.
— [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/carbon)

## 🪙 Metals

**Copper's backwardation signals a tight physical market.** The metal rose to about **$6.44/lb** (+2.5% on the day) on 30 July, with nearby LME contracts trading roughly $24/tonne above three-month futures — the Fed's steady hand and persistent AI/grid-driven demand outweighed Beijing's reluctance to add fresh stimulus.
*Risk lens:* backwardation this pronounced usually points to near-term physical scarcity rather than paper positioning — spread and delivery risk on short-dated copper exposure is elevated.
— [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/copper) · [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/copper-price-jumps-on-tightening-supply-mining-stocks-rally/)

**Gold holds near $4,070–4,130/oz**, up on the Fed's hawkish hold and continued safe-haven demand, though still well below January's 2026 record above $5,590.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/select/the-price-of-gold-today-july-30-2026/)

## 📊 Macro & cross-asset

**The Fed holds at 3.50–3.75% — with a rare hawkish split.** Wednesday's 9-3 vote saw three regional presidents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) dissent in favour of higher rates, the first time since September 2016 that three policymakers have broken ranks in the same direction; the 10-year Treasury yield rose 5bp to **4.657%**.
*Risk lens:* a visibly divided committee raises the odds of a policy surprise in either direction — rate-vol pricing looks cheap relative to the disagreement on display.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/fed-rate-decision-july-2026.html) · [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a.htm)

**Equities whipsaw around the decision.** The Dow fell more than 840 points (-1.6%) on Wednesday's hawkish hold, then rebounded Thursday — S&P 500 +1.7% to 7,437.63, Nasdaq +2.8% to 25,122.18 (snapping a six-day losing streak), Dow +1.2% to 52,208.06 — as Microsoft's results and chip names drove the bounce.
*Risk lens:* a four-figure, two-day Dow round trip around a single Fed meeting is a reminder that gamma risk around rate decisions hasn't gone away just because rates stayed on hold.
— [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/fed-rate-decision-july-2026.html) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)
