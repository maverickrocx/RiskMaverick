*Sourced from Bloomberg's Energy Daily.*

**More than 80 carbon policies are now in force worldwide, and the bill is
scheduled to roughly double within a decade.** BloombergNEF puts compliance
costs at $96 billion in 2026, rising to $188 billion in 2035, and a cumulative
$1.4 trillion over the next ten years. The obligations reach across sectors
from oil and gas to utilities, requiring firms either to cut emissions or buy
allowances. BNEF's own framing is that with energy prices likely to stay high,
carbon fees will increasingly pit decarbonisation against affordability.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-10/trump-s-low-key-pivot-leaves-hormuz-oil-in-limbo)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (compliance demand), bearish (policy durability)** — a
cost curve that steepens into the 2030s is structurally bullish allowance
demand. But the affordability collision BNEF flags is precisely the political
mechanism by which carbon schemes get diluted mid-life. For anyone carrying
multi-year allowance length, the risk is not the price path — it is regulatory
amendment, which no forward hedge protects against.
