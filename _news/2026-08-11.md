## Oil / Products

*Sourced from Bloomberg's Energy Daily.*

**Oil erased its gains after Pakistan signalled the US and Iran were close to
an arrangement.** The report was enough to unwind the day's move, in a market
that has spent months repricing on each turn in the negotiation rather than on
barrels.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-aug-11)

*Risk lens:* **Neutral, with headline risk dominating** — when flat price is
set by third-party diplomatic commentary rather than physical balances,
stop-losses on fundamental positions get triggered by noise. Size positions to
survive the rumour, not the forecast.

**Chinese teapot refiners are running their stockpiles down, which puts a floor
under Iranian sales.** Oil inventories in Shandong — home to most of China's
independent refiners — fell to an eight-month low in July, according to Energy
Aspects data. Those refiners are by far the largest buyers of Iranian crude,
and thinning stocks may spur fresh purchases, giving Tehran a boost after sales
slowed.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/china-s-teapots-may-boost-iran-oil-buying-as-stockpiles-dwindle)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (Iranian export volumes), bearish (sanctions premium)** —
Chinese restocking is the release valve that has kept Iranian barrels moving
throughout the blockade. It weakens the economic-pressure strategy Washington
is now relying on, and it argues against pricing a sustained supply loss from
Iran into the back of the curve.

**A drone strike hit a refinery in western Libya, destroying a storage tank.**
The attack put wider oil facilities at risk and threatens to aggravate fuel
shortages in the OPEC member.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/drone-hits-tank-at-libyan-refinery-threatening-oil-facilities)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (Mediterranean product)** — another increment of
refining capacity taken offline in a basin already short of distillate. Small
in isolation; meaningful as the latest in a run of outages that have removed
optionality from the product barrel.

## Gas & Power

*Sourced from Bloomberg's Energy Daily.*

**Wind-lease cancellations are being converted into gas investment by
contract.** Refunds to developers for cancelled US offshore leases now total
$4 billion, but much of that cash is not going back to investors — it is being
redirected into fossil-fuel projects as a condition of settlement. RWE settled
with the US government last week to relinquish leases off New York, California
and Louisiana, and as part of that agreement pledged $900 million for a stake
in Woodside's Louisiana LNG export facility. TotalEnergies struck a comparable
deal earlier this year, getting back almost $1 billion while committing to US
gas and LNG. Bluepoint Wind, part-owned by BlackRock, gave up a New York
development, with BlackRock's Global Infrastructure Partners directing as much
as $765 million into US LNG infrastructure.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-11/trump-s-anti-wind-crusade-is-boosting-us-gas) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-refunds-4-billion-to-cancel-us-offshore-wind-projects) · [US Interior](https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/interior-announces-two-historic-agreements-promote-affordable-reliable-energy)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (US gas demand), bearish (renewables build-out)** — this
is policy risk expressing itself through settlement terms rather than
legislation, which makes it far harder to hedge or forecast. For a generation
book it shifts the long-run US fuel mix toward gas; for anyone holding
renewable development exposure it establishes that a lease is a revocable
asset, and that the compensation may arrive with strings attached.

**The gas momentum lands on production that is already at record levels.** US
output is running at highs just as Big Tech turns to the fuel to meet surging
data-centre power demand. LNG gets a lift at the same time, with the Iran war
throttling Persian Gulf flows and lifting global prices, making US supply look
increasingly attractive to international buyers.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-11/trump-s-anti-wind-crusade-is-boosting-us-gas) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-29/meta-s-need-for-gas-power-boosts-entergy-spending-by-14-billion)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (Henry Hub basis)** — record supply meeting data-centre
load growth and export pull at the same time is a basis story, not a flat-price
one. The risk sits in where the gas is versus where the new demand is, and
pipeline constraints between the two are what will price.

## LNG

*Sourced from Bloomberg's Energy Daily.*

**India is pulling South Asian LNG imports sharply higher even with Hormuz
traffic stalled.** As of 9 August, its cumulative August imports were 131%
above the same period in 2025, according to BloombergNEF. The likely driver is
substitution rather than appetite: war-related disruption has tightened propane
supply, pushing industrial users onto natural gas.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-11/trump-s-anti-wind-crusade-is-boosting-us-gas)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (JKM), and watch the correlation** — demand created by
scarcity in a *different* fuel does not behave like ordinary demand growth. It
appears fast, it is price-insensitive while the substitution holds, and it
reverses just as fast if propane supply normalises. Anyone modelling Indian LNG
offtake off economic growth is using the wrong driver, and term positions
written against this volume carry reversal risk that the forward curve is not
pricing.

**Meanwhile the US supply side keeps being reinforced by policy.** Settlements
over cancelled offshore wind leases are directing developer capital into US LNG
export infrastructure — RWE into Woodside's Louisiana project, BlackRock's
Global Infrastructure Partners committing up to $765 million.
— [Woodside](https://www.woodside.com/what-we-do/growth-projects/woodside-louisiana-lng) · [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/environment-and-energy/rwe-settles-with-us-on-offshore-wind-leases-for-1-22-billion)

*Risk lens:* **Bearish (long-dated JKM–TTF spread)** — more US export capacity
arriving into a market where Atlantic-basin supply is already growing narrows
the arbitrage that makes diversion economics work. The front stays tight; the
back end is where this shows up.
