*Sourced from this morning's Bloomberg newsletter.*

**The yen's intervention-driven rally is fading fast.** The currency traded
around 158.40 per dollar on Friday, well off the 155.23 high reached
Monday and down from an intervention that had pulled it back from a
near-four-decade low around 164. The BOJ is estimated to have spent some
$87 billion over two days last week on the first joint yen-buying
operation with the US since 1998. Overnight index swaps imply about a 60%
chance of a BOJ rate hike by September, but the structural forces pushing
the yen down — the US rate differential, Japan's debt load, its role as a
favoured carry-trade funding currency — haven't changed.
*Risk lens:* an intervention that's already surrendered half its gains
inside a week is a weak floor, not a new equilibrium. Carry trades funded
in yen are still running the same depreciation risk the intervention was
meant to remove, just with a shorter fuse before the next round of
official buying — or the next leg down.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/yen-surrenders-nearly-half-its-gains-from-us-japan-intervention)

**The US used euros, not dollars, to fund the yen intervention — and never
told the ECB in advance.** The Financial Times reports the US sold euros to
buy yen without informing Frankfurt until after the July 31 trade had been
executed; ECB President Christine Lagarde and Treasury Secretary Scott
Bessent spoke about it only days later. Some senior ECB officials view the
US's use of euros, rather than dollars, as an unprecedented breach of
longstanding convention between western monetary authorities.
*Risk lens:* if one currency can be drawn into another country's
intervention without warning, EUR/USD and EUR/JPY hedges built on the
assumption that G7 central banks coordinate transparently need a second
look — the correlation between "official FX action" and "advance
notice to affected central banks" just broke.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/us-sale-of-euros-for-yen-intervention-blindsided-europe-ft-says)
