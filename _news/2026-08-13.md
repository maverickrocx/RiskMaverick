## Gas & Power

*Sourced from Bloomberg's Green Daily.*

**Europe's most important inland waterway is running dry with no relief in
sight.** Levels at Kaub, the Rhine's shallowest point, have fallen to the
lowest since records began in 1880, and may drop further as another heat wave
bakes the continent this week. Forecasters say weeks of sustained rain would be
needed to return the river to normal, meaning the crisis could drag into
October. The Rhine carries freight through Europe's industrial heartland from
Switzerland to Rotterdam, and the disruption is already reaching steelmaker
Thyssenkrupp, BASF and Lanxess. Lanxess said vessels are carrying significantly
lighter loads and that with some loading points inaccessible it is switching to
rail and road where it can, calling the situation "highly dynamic".
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/rhine-river-at-record-low-levels-is-forcing-industry-to-react)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (German power and industrial input costs)** — low Rhine
levels hit energy twice: barge-delivered coal and fuel oil get more expensive
per tonne as vessels part-load, and the same heat suppressing the river cuts
hydro and thermal cooling capacity. This is a logistics constraint expressing
itself as a power-price and industrial-margin event, and it lands on a German
gas position already behind on storage. For anyone with delivered-basis supply
contracts on the Rhine corridor, freight is the exposure that reprices first —
and it is rarely the one that is hedged.

## Carbon

*Sourced from Bloomberg's Green Daily.*

**On cleaner jet fuel, the stick is beating the carrot.** European airlines
pulled well ahead on sustainable aviation fuel last year while US and Asian
carriers fell further behind, according to a Bloomberg News analysis of dozens
of airline environmental filings. IAG, the British Airways parent, led
passenger airlines at 3.3% of its jet fuel from SAF, followed by
Air France-KLM at 2.9% and Ryanair at 2.0%. US airlines ran between 0.3% and
1%. The gap tracks policy: EU and UK mandates requiring 2% SAF kicked in last
year, with the UK obligation stepping up to 3.6% this year, while the US has
avoided mandates amid airline opposition and relies on production incentives
instead. With SAF costing two to three times conventional fuel, voluntary
demand has stayed weak. IAG alone bought more than 99 million gallons against
roughly 97 million consumed by all major US carriers.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/europe-widens-lead-in-cleaner-jet-fuel-as-us-asia-fall-behind) · [IATA](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-12-09-04/)

*Risk lens:* **Bullish (EU/UK SAF obligation cost)** — a stepping mandate
against supply that industry groups themselves call concerning is a squeeze
with a scheduled start date. SAF was just 0.6% of global jet fuel consumption
in 2025, so the compliance pool is thin relative to the obligation curve.
Airlines carrying unhedged mandate exposure into a rising percentage are short
a physically constrained commodity.

**A dispute over how to count forest carbon has cost a standard-setter its
technical lead.** Tim Searchinger of Princeton is leaving his role as technical
director of land-related issues at the World Resources Institute over what he
describes as shortcomings in the Greenhouse Gas Protocol's process for drafting
new rules. Two camps are split between activity-based accounting, which
separates human-caused change from natural processes, and the managed land
proxy, which treats any change on managed forest land as human-caused. Critics
warn the latter would let companies double count removals and claim credit for
carbon stored naturally.
— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/dispute-over-counting-forest-carbon-roils-global-standard-setter)

*Risk lens:* **Bearish (forestry-based credits)** — the methodology is the
asset. An unresolved split at the standard-setter, with a senior technical
resignation attached, is exactly the sort of governance event that reprices
existing forestry credit inventory. Anyone holding those credits against a
future compliance obligation is carrying methodology risk that no price hedge
covers.
