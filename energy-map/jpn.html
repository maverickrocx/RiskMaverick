Japan

The world's second-largest LNG buyer, with regional utilities and a growing power exchange. In 2026 the Qatari outage pushed Japanese wholesale power up about 40%, while the first TEPCO reactor since Fukushima came back.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Power up about 40%. The JEPX system price averaged ¥16.1/kWh so far this year, against ¥11.5 in the same period of 2025, and over ¥26 in early November.
  • Steady climb. Monthly averages rose every quarter, from about ¥11 in January to ¥22.5 in October.
  • LNG is watched closely. METI said on 4 November the winter supply situation is not immediately tight, and asked utilities to keep buying steadily (METI).
  • Nuclear is returning. Kashiwazaki-Kariwa unit 6 entered commercial operation in April 2026, TEPCO's first since 2011 (JAIF).
  • Carbon trading starts. The mandatory GX-ETS began in April 2026 for companies emitting over 100,000 tonnes a year (METI).
The call

Japanese power is an LNG trade with a nuclear hedge. Every reactor that restarts cuts LNG demand; every month the Qatari outage continues keeps spot cargoes expensive.

  • Two frequencies. East Japan runs at 50 Hz and the west at 60 Hz, with limited transfer between them, so area prices can split.
  • A growing traded market. Hedge funds and high-frequency traders are entering Japanese power futures, though volumes are still a fraction of Germany's (Bloomberg).

04

Prices

  • A rising curve all year. Unlike Europe's summer dip, Japanese prices rose almost every month as spot LNG costs fed through.
  • Winter risk. Cold weather in the east, where Tokyo sits, can split area prices from the system price.

Source: JEPX day-ahead system price; our simple monthly averages.
