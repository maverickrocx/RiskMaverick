Norway

A hydro system that exports power and gas to Europe. In 2026 continental gas prices pulled southern Norwegian power to its highest since 2022, while the north stayed cheap.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Southern prices up about 80%. NO2 averaged about €115/MWh so far this year against €64 in 2025; in the week to 9 November it hit 217 øre/kWh, the highest weekly average since late 2022 (NVE).
  • Less water, fewer exports. Hydro output fell about 9% and net power exports dropped from about 21 TWh to 6 TWh in the year to mid-November.
  • North and south split. Northern Norway (NO4) averaged just 19 øre/kWh in the same week, cut off from the south by grid bottlenecks (NVE).
  • Households are shielded. The Norgespris scheme lets households fix power at 40 øre/kWh before VAT through 2026 (Government of Norway).
  • Europe's gas supplier. Norway's pipelines are now Europe's most important single gas source, and their winter availability matters more than ever.
The call

Southern Norway prices off the continent through its cables, and its reservoirs decide how much it can export. Producers have been running water down at high prices; a cold, dry late winter would leave less to sell when Europe needs it most.

Market snapshot

ItemNorway
Market modelZonal pool (five bidding zones NO1–NO5), part of the Nordic market and coupled with Europe
Power exchangeNord Pool, EPEX SPOT; Nasdaq for derivatives
System operatorStatnett
RegulatorNVE (Norwegian Energy Regulatory Authority)
GasProducer and exporter; Gassco runs the export pipelines; no domestic gas market to speak of
  • Five zones, very different prices. The south is coupled to Germany, the Netherlands and Britain; the north is not.
  • Water is the fuel. Reservoir hydro lets producers choose when to generate, so their view of future prices sets today's price.

  • Hydro is almost everything: about 88% of generation.

  • Exports go where prices are highest: mainly Britain, Germany and Denmark.
04

Prices

  • The south imports the continent's gas price. NO2 monthly averages ran about €130/MWh in August and September and €140 in October, against €60 in October 2025; early November averaged about €218/MWh.
  • Still cheaper than Germany. In the week to 9 November German prices were 50–80 øre/kWh above southern Norway (NVE).
  • Producers spent water early. NVE notes prices above what the market expects for winter led some producers to run water they might have saved (NVE).

Source: Energy-Charts, day-ahead NO2; our time-weighted averages.
