Spain

Solar is now Spain's largest source of power, and it keeps Iberian prices among the lowest in Europe. In 2026 the gas shock still reached Spain's evenings, and the government kept its oldest nuclear plant open to 2030.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Still one of Europe's cheapest markets. About €88/MWh so far this year, against €120 in Germany and €160 in northern Italy, though 37% above 2025.
  • Solar is the largest source, up about a fifth on 2025, and it pushes more hours below zero than last year.
  • Gas still sets the evening price. Monthly averages rose from about €118/MWh in August to €167 in October as gas set the price after sunset.
  • Almaraz stays open. The government extended both Almaraz reactors to 8 June 2030, citing uncertainty in international energy markets (BOE).
  • More LNG in reserve. The winter plan now requires 21 days of LNG in terminal tanks, up from 16.5 (La Moncloa).
The call

Spanish power is a solar-shaped curve: very cheap middays, gas-priced evenings. The daily spread, not the average, is where the risk and the return sit. Winter, with less sun, is when the gas link shows most.

  • An energy island. Interconnection with France is small, so Iberian prices often decouple from the rest of Europe.
  • An LNG-led gas system. Spain relies on LNG terminals and pipeline gas from Algeria rather than storage.

04

Prices

  • A price shaped by the sun. February averaged just €17/MWh on wind, hydro and solar; prices rose through the year as gas took the evening hours.
  • Autumn climb. Monthly averages ran about €118/MWh in August, €143 in September and €167 in October, against €76 in October 2025; early November averaged about €197/MWh.
  • More negative prices: 698 hours so far, against 552 in the same period of 2025.

Source: Energy-Charts, OMIE day-ahead ES; our averages.
