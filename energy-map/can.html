Canada

Ten provincial power systems, mostly hydro and nuclear, and one of the world's largest gas producers. In 2026 Canada sold its first full year of LNG to Asia while Alberta power got cheaper, not dearer.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Alberta power is cheaper. The pool price averaged about C$34/MWh so far this year, 23% below 2025.
  • Gas is cheap at home. Western Canadian gas trades at a discount to Henry Hub; LNG exports from Kitimat have not yet changed that.
  • Ontario's demand outlook is steep. IESO forecasts 152 TWh in 2026 rising to 250 TWh by 2050, with an energy gap from 2032 (IESO).
  • Alberta is redesigning its market. The Restructured Energy Market is due to start being implemented in 2027 (AESO).
  • Nuclear rebuild continues. Darlington's refurbishment completes in 2026; Bruce and Pickering follow in 2033–34 (IESO).
The call

Canada is a long-gas, long-hydro market insulated from the global LNG shock. The risks are domestic: Alberta's market redesign, Ontario's demand growth and the timing of nuclear refurbishments.

04

Prices

  • Alberta was cheap all year. Monthly pool prices ranged from about C$18/MWh in June to C$52 in October.
  • Far from the LNG shock. Unlike Europe and Asia, Canadian power prices are set by domestic gas and local supply and demand.

Source: AESO historical pool price; our monthly averages.
