France

Europe's largest low-carbon generator and biggest power exporter. Its nuclear fleet still caps prices most hours, but in 2026 the hours it cannot cover are priced off Gulf LNG.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Power is about 60% dearer than in 2025 so far this year, one of the biggest rises among Europe's large markets, because 2025 was unusually cheap and the marginal hours in 2026 are priced off gas.
  • Nuclear held up. About 309 TWh from 1 January to 11 November, level with 2025, inside EDF's 350–370 TWh guidance for the year (EDF).
  • Summer was the warning. Heat and drought took up to 13.4 GW of reactors offline on 17 August, and hydro stocks ended August at the lowest level for the time of year since at least 2015 (RTE).
  • Still a big exporter. Net exports of about 80 TWh so far, led by Italy, Great Britain and Germany.
  • ARENH is gone. The new revenue-sharing scheme (VNU) is set at zero for 2026, because EDF's nuclear revenue is estimated below the €78/MWh threshold (CRE).
The call

French winter power is a bet on reactor availability and cold. With the fleet running, France exports its way to prices below Germany's. Lose a few gigawatts in a cold snap, and France imports German and Belgian gas-priced power instead.

  • Demand is flat, but very weather-sensitive: France heats with electricity, so cold adds load fast.
  • Nuclear is the only big variable. Output is level with 2025 despite summer heat losses that RTE put at up to about 10 TWh, a record (RTE).
  • Hydro is weak. Summer output was the second lowest in 50 years and reservoirs ended August at 2.1 TWh, a record low for the date. RTE does not see that as a winter supply threat on its own.
  • Gas plants run for export. RTE notes French gas-fired output in summer was mostly used to export power.
04

Prices

  • Nuclear sets the floor, gas sets the peaks. Most hours clear well below German prices. When reactors are short or neighbours need power, France imports the continental gas price.
  • Spring was cheap, autumn was not. April averaged about €40/MWh. From August the price followed gas: €123 in August, €143 in September, €166 in October.
  • November spike. The first eleven days averaged about €265/MWh, close to German levels, as continental gas set the price on both sides of the border.
  • Negative prices are still common: 535 hours so far, against 509 in the same period of 2025, as solar grows into a nuclear-heavy system.

Source: Energy-Charts, EPEX SPOT day-ahead FR; our time-weighted calculation.
