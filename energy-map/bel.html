Belgium

A small, industrial, import-dependent market at the crossroads of north-west European gas. In 2026 its two remaining reactors spent half the year offline, and the state moved to buy the whole nuclear fleet.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Power is about 47% dearer than in 2025 so far this year, one of the steepest rises in north-west Europe.
  • Nuclear went missing. Belgium's two operating reactors produced nothing from April to September; output fell from about 20 TWh to 6 TWh in the year to mid-November, and resumed in October (Energy-Charts).
  • Imports filled the gap. Net imports rose from about 12 TWh to 22 TWh, mostly from the Netherlands and France, and gas-fired output rose by more than a fifth.
  • The state wants the fleet. Belgium and Engie signed a letter of intent in April for the state to take over all seven reactors; the deadline for heads of terms has moved to 12 December (Engie, World Nuclear News).
  • Zeebrugge matters more than ever as an LNG entry point for north-west Europe.
The call

With both reactors back online, Belgium's winter is a spread trade against the Netherlands and France. It imports when it is short and pays their price; a reactor trip in a cold week would make it the dearest market in the region.

04

Prices

  • Belgium imports its price. As a net importer, it usually clears at or above the Dutch and French price in the same hour.
  • Monthly averages rose from about €129/MWh in August to €156 in September and €174 in October; the first part of November averaged about €267/MWh.
  • Fewer negative prices: 312 hours so far, against 520 in the same period of 2025.

Source: Energy-Charts, EPEX SPOT day-ahead BE; our averages.
