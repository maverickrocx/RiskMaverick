United States

The world's largest gas producer and LNG exporter, with power priced node by node across seven grid operators. In 2026 the US gained from the Middle East gas shock: cheap Henry Hub gas, record LNG exports, and power prices driven by data-centre demand rather than fuel.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Cheap gas at home. Henry Hub averaged about $3.42/MMBtu so far this year and EIA expects $2.87 this winter, 36% below last winter, while international prices are at their highest since 2022 (EIA).
  • Record LNG exports. EIA forecasts 17.6 Bcf/d in 2026 and 19.0 Bcf/d in 2027, up from 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025, with terminals running near full capacity (EIA).
  • Power is up anyway. PJM West peak power averaged about $94/MWh, 60% above 2025, with sharp spikes in January and July.
  • Capacity is short. PJM's 2028/29 auction cleared at the $325/MW-day cap and still fell 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement (PJM).
  • Storage is comfortable. 3,801 Bcf at 30 October, 1.4% above the five-year average (EIA).
The call

US risk is in power, not gas. Henry Hub is cushioned by above-average storage and a warm El Niño outlook, but capacity markets are short and data-centre load keeps growing. Basis and capacity prices carry the risk, not the fuel.

04

Prices

  • Spikes, not a trend. PJM West peak averaged about $191/MWh in January and $157 in July, against $58–94 in other months.
  • Gas is cheap. Henry Hub fell from $7.72 in January to under $3 for most of the year.
  • Capacity is the expensive part. PJM's capacity price of $325/MW-day is now a large share of the delivered power cost.

Power: EIA's daily ICE wholesale prices, PJM West real-time peak; gas: EIA Henry Hub spot. Our monthly averages.
