Denmark

The world's wind-power pioneer, wired to Norway, Sweden, Germany, the Netherlands and Britain. In 2026 wind supplied over half of its power, but its prices still followed the continent's gas shock.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Western Denmark up about 48% so far this year, to about €118/MWh, close to German levels.
  • Wind supplies over half of generation and solar about a sixth; coal is almost gone.
  • Gas storage is in good shape. 88.5% full on 11 November, above the five-year average of 82.7% (GIE AGSI+).
  • The storage target was cut to 75%, because Denmark is a net gas exporter with North Sea output and biogas (Danish Energy Agency).
  • Fewer negative prices: 208 hours against 441 a year earlier.
The call

Danish power is a wind spread between Norway's hydro and Germany's gas. In windy weeks it exports at low prices; in still weeks it imports German prices.

04

Prices

  • DK1 tracks Germany. Monthly averages rose to about €160/MWh in October against €78 a year earlier; early November averaged about €272/MWh.
  • Wind sets the low end. Windy hours clear at or below zero; still hours import continental prices.

Source: Energy-Charts, day-ahead DK1; our time-weighted averages.
