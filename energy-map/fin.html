Finland

Nuclear, wind and hydro, with one price zone and almost no gas in the power mix. In 2026 Finland had some of Europe's cheapest power, but it is exposed to a single large reactor and its links with Sweden and Estonia.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Among Europe's cheapest power: about €58/MWh so far this year, though 45% above a very cheap 2025.
  • Nuclear holds steady at about 38% of generation; wind supplies about 27%.
  • Negative prices nearly vanished: 56 hours against 439 a year earlier.
  • Winter outlook stable. Fingrid expects enough power if large plants and the links with Sweden and Estonia work normally, with demand up to about 15,200 MW on a very cold, still day (Fingrid).
  • Gas comes by sea. The Inkoo floating LNG terminal can regasify over 40 TWh a year (Gasgrid).
The call

Finnish power is cheap until something big trips. One large reactor or one interconnector failure in a cold, still week is what sets the winter risk.

RegulatorEnergy Authority (Energiavirasto)

  • Russia is gone. Finland has had no electricity or gas trade with Russia since 2022.

04

Prices

  • Cheap middle of the year. July averaged about €15/MWh; October about €38, below October 2025.
  • Winter is the risk. January and February averaged €117 and €137/MWh, and early November about €69.

Source: Energy-Charts, day-ahead FI; our time-weighted averages.
