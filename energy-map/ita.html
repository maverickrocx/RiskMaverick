Pool clearing Last reviewed 13 November 2026

Italy

Europe's most gas-dependent large power market, where gas plants set the price in most hours. In 2026 that made Italian power the dearest in western Europe, and its largest Qatari LNG contract has been under force majeure since April.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • The dearest power in western Europe. The northern zone averaged about €160/MWh so far this year, 38% above 2025, and close to €280/MWh in early November.
  • Gas does the work. Gas-fired plants supplied almost half of generation and their output rose about 16% as hydro fell about a fifth.
  • Qatari LNG is missing. QatarEnergy has extended force majeure on Edison's cargoes from April to the end of December 2026; Edison says it has replaced most of them through Adriatic LNG (Edison).
  • Storage is the bright spot. 87.9% full on 11 November, close to the five-year average of 93.9% and far better placed than Germany or the Netherlands (GIE AGSI+).
  • Imports remain large. Italy is a net importer of power, mainly from France and Switzerland.
The call

Italian power is a gas spread with a carbon add-on. Full storage protects volumes this winter; it does not protect price, because the marginal molecule is still bought against Asian LNG buyers and TTF.

  • Hydro failed. The same western European drought that drained French reservoirs cut Italian hydro by about a fifth.
  • Gas filled the gap, at crisis prices, which is why Italian power rose more in euros than any of its neighbours.
  • Demand is growing about 4%, against flat demand in Germany and France.
04

Prices

  • Gas sets the price most hours. Unlike Spain or France, Italy has too little cheap baseload to push gas out of the stack.
  • Monthly averages in the North zone rose from about €177/MWh in August to €206 in September and €236 in October, against €112 in October 2025. The first part of November averaged about €279/MWh.
  • No negative prices in the North zone this year or last.

Source: Energy-Charts, day-ahead IT-North; our averages.
