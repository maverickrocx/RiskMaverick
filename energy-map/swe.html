Sweden

Hydro, nuclear and wind, split into four price zones from the Arctic to Malmö. In 2026 lower nuclear and hydro output and the continental gas shock pushed southern prices up sharply.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Stockholm-zone power up about 70% so far this year, to about €75/MWh, though still far below Germany.
  • Nuclear output down about 12% and hydro about 6% in the year to mid-November.
  • A strategic reserve is back. Svenska kraftnät has contracted 330 MW at Karlshamn for 16 November to 15 March, citing low reservoirs and low European gas stocks (Svenska kraftnät).
  • Still a big exporter, mainly to Finland, Denmark and Norway, but less than last year.
  • Far fewer negative prices: 90 hours against 314 a year earlier.
The call

Swedish risk sits in the south. SE3 and SE4 import continental prices when wind drops and reactors are offline; the north stays cheap behind grid bottlenecks.

04

Prices

  • A late-year jump. SE3 averaged about €74/MWh in October, against €57 a year earlier; the first part of November averaged about €181/MWh.
  • The south imports the continent. When wind is low, SE3 and SE4 clear close to German prices.
  • Fewer negative hours as higher continental prices pulled more Swedish power south.

Source: Energy-Charts, day-ahead SE3; our time-weighted averages.
