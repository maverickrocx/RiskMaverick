United Kingdom

A single national power price set by gas most hours, with gas that arrives by Norwegian pipeline or LNG tanker. In 2026 Britain imports the global LNG shock directly into its power price.

SUMMARY

At a glance

  • Wholesale power is about 38% dearer than in 2025 so far this year, and gas at the NBP is more than double its level of a year ago.
  • Gas sets the price. Gas plants supplied about 30% of generation this year; wind about 34%. When wind drops, the marginal molecule is LNG or Norwegian pipeline gas.
  • Supply is adequate on paper. National Gas expects positive margins this winter even in severe cases, and NESO puts the electricity de-rated margin at 5.9 GW (National Gas, NESO).
  • Bills are rising. Ofgem raised the price cap 4% for October to December 2026, to £1,723 a year for a typical household, with gas bills up 8% (Ofgem).
  • Imports cover the gap. Britain is a net importer of power, mainly from France and Norway.
The call

British winter power is a spread trade against Europe. With little gas storage, Britain buys its marginal gas spot. Strong wind and full French interconnectors cap prices; a still, cold week with low French exports does not.

Market snapshot

ItemGreat Britain
Market modelBilateral trading plus day-ahead auctions; one national price zone; half-hourly settlement
Power exchangesN2EX (Nord Pool) and EPEX SPOT
System operatorNESO, publicly owned and independent since October 2024; electricity and gas
SettlementElexon (Balancing and Settlement Code)
Gas hubNational Balancing Point (NBP), quoted in pence per therm
Gas transmissionNational Gas Transmission
Regulator and policyOfgem; Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ)
  • One price, no zones. The government rejected zonal pricing in 2025 and kept a reformed national price (DESNZ). Constraint costs sit in network charges, not the spot price.
  • What gets built is administered. The Capacity Market and Contracts for Difference decide new build; the spot market decides what runs today.

04

Prices

  • Gas sets the price most hours. A gas turbine is usually the marginal unit, so British power tracks NBP plus carbon.
  • The second half of 2026 broke away. The market index averaged about £130/MWh in August and September and £151/MWh in October. The first part of November averaged about £213/MWh.
  • Gas more than doubled. The NBP System Average Price averaged about £72/MWh in October, against £27/MWh in October 2025.
  • Fewer negative prices: 174 hours so far, against 244 in the same period of 2025.
  • Carbon adds cost. UK allowances averaged £63.94/t in October, up from £52.41 in May (UK ETS Authority).

Power: Elexon Market Index Data; gas: National Gas Transmission via ONS. Our averages.
