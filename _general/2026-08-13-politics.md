**Parliament adjourned sine die with the legislation through and the debate
largely absent.** The Monsoon Session, which began on 20 July and ran 19
sittings over 25 days, passed 12 bills — among them the Mines and Minerals
(Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, the Tribunals Reforms Bill and the
Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill. Productivity
told the other story: roughly 19% in the Lok Sabha and 39% in the Rajya Sabha,
with 11 of the Lok Sabha's bills passing without discussion. Rajya Sabha
Chairman C P Radhakrishnan closed by saying members had not made full use of
the opportunities available to raise matters of public concern. Opposition
parties boycotted the Lok Sabha Speaker's customary tea gathering afterwards,
though Kharge and Sonia Gandhi attended the Rajya Sabha equivalent.

**Tamil Nadu has moved first on delimitation.** Chief Minister Vijay wrote to
the Prime Minister citing a state Assembly resolution passed on 12 August,
asking that the Lok Sabha's total strength be permanently fixed at 543 seats
and that the current distribution among states remain unchanged. The proposed
Delimitation Bill would expand the House to 850 seats on fresh census data,
reviving southern states' fear that fast-growing northern states gain at their
expense. The resolution argues that states which successfully implemented
population control should not be penalised with reduced representation, and
separately urges that the one-third reservation for women be implemented in the
next Lok Sabha election without being tied to delimitation.

**Kerala is contesting one of the bills that just passed.** The state says the
Mines and Minerals Act amendments erode its constitutional powers and cost it
revenue by restricting states from levying taxes on mineral-bearing land,
calling it an encroachment on fiscal autonomy. It plans to register a formal
protest and explore legal remedies.
